    ## Fredholm index of an elliptic operator
    The idea of index of a differential operators goes back to the very basic question in differential equations: when is the equation $Pu=f$ solved for $P$ is a differential operator? And if the equation can be solved, is the solution unique? 

    The answer for the first question is when $f$ is in the range of $P$. For the uniqueness, the equation is uniquely solved if the kernel of $P$ is $\{0}$. In other words, $\ker P$ is the obstruction to the uniqueness of the equation. Similarly, if　$P$ has a realization $P: X \rightarrow Y$, the $\cokernel P : = Y \setminus \range P$ is the obstruction to the solvability. When the dimensions of $\ker P$ and $\cokernel P$ are finite, we encode them into a unique quantity called index of $P$
    $$ \ind P := \dim \ker P - \dim \cokernel P. $$

    Therefore, the index of $P$ in some sense measures how far it is to the unique solvability of $P$.
